The short version

why offcuts cost less

A full slab is bigger than most jobs need. What is left over after a kitchen is still the same stone, the same thickness and the same finish — it is just a smaller piece. If your job fits one, you pay a fraction of the slab price for it.

Same stone, smaller piece

Identical material and finish to the full slab it came off. Nothing about the quality changes.

A fraction of slab price

You are not paying for stone you will never use, and there is no slab to order in.

Ready now

It is already on the rack, so there is no wait on a supplier delivery.

One piece, one price

Cut, polished edges and install quoted together. No surprises at the end.

Jobs that fit an offcut

what people use them for

If your piece is under about 1.5 metres, there is a good chance something on the rack will do the job.

1

Vanity tops

The most common offcut job. Cut to fit, with the basin and tap holes done.

2

Hall & side tables

A long narrow remnant makes a stone top that would cost far more from a slab.

3

Splashbacks

Behind a cooktop or along a laundry trough, in the same stone as your bench.

4

Shelves & hearths

Floating shelves, fireplace hearths and window sills.
